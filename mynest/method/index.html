The MyNest method

MyNest is a bounded environmental and behavioural decision framework for families observing an own-room transition in children aged 2½–6. It helps a family locate the main barrier, let the child choose one safe room element, change one thing, and observe what happens for 14 days. It is not medical treatment, sleep therapy, or a promise that a child will sleep independently.

Who it is for

MyNest is for a parent or caregiver who already has a safe, usable sleep space and is considering a gentle own-room transition within the next 14 days. It is designed for structured observation, not for enforcing separation.

Who it is not for

It is not the right tool when the room is unsafe or when breathing concerns, significant pain, severe distress, persistent disruptive nightmares or night terrors, strong daytime fear, or major developmental or medical concerns may be involved. In those cases, pause the experiment and seek appropriate qualified guidance. Read the full boundary.

The five steps

  1. Listen. Locate when resistance begins and choose the closest observation category.
  2. Choose. Let the child choose one approved light, story or sound, or room friend while the adult controls safety and use.
  3. Prepare. Keep the room and bedtime sequence familiar, changing one variable only.
  4. Try. Treat room entry, a partial night, and a full night as separate observations.
  5. Observe. Record structured outcomes at Day 1, 3, 7, and 14 before deciding what to change next.

F01–F06 observation categories

These codes describe where resistance may be occurring; they are not diagnoses.

  • F01 · Darkness or imagined threatsLight, shadows, or low-light uncertainty
  • F02 · SeparationDifficulty when the caregiver moves away
  • F03 · Low room ownershipThe room does not yet feel chosen or personally meaningful
  • F04 · Environmental frictionNoise, temperature, bedding, layout, glare, or another physical cue
  • F05 · Unpredictable transitionThe bedtime steps or responses keep changing
  • F06 · Unclear or non-room causeThe pattern is uncertain or may sit outside the room experiment

Two rules keep the experiment interpretable

One-change rule: do not change the bed, light, routine, caregiver presence, and comfort objects at the same time. Choose the smallest plausible change and keep the rest steady.

Child-choice rule: the child chooses within a small set of adult-approved options. The adult retains control of electrical safety, brightness, placement, volume, duration, age suitability, and the decision to pause.

What the 14-day observation measures

OWN_ROOM_ATTEMPT
The child entered or began the own-room sleep attempt, whether or not sleep followed.
FULL_OWN_ROOM_NIGHT
The child slept the full night in their own room.
PARTIAL_OWN_ROOM_NIGHT
The child slept part, but not all, of the night in their own room.
VOLUNTARY_ROOM_ENTRY
The child entered without coercion; the interface records whether entry was easy, supported, or did not occur.
RETURN_DESIRE
In the Magic Room pilot, whether the child asks or chooses to return after first exposure.
ROOM_GRAVITY
An internal pilot label for patterns of voluntary entry, time in the room, identification with it, and return desire. It is not a validated clinical measure or a sleep-outcome claim.

Attempts include full nights, partial nights, and entered-only observations. Partial nights are never counted as full nights.

Evidence status and limitations

Pilot evidence is not yet sufficient.

As of 13 October 2026, no real family has completed the production pilot and no public case has passed the evidence gate. Current observations are parent-reported, household-specific, and cannot establish clinical effectiveness or predict success.

Anonymous pilot data may be aggregated only after completion, consent, privacy, and evidence checks pass. Test records are not evidence. See the Evidence Registry for current counts and protocol status.
