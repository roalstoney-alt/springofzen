# 1. North Star

The room must look believable even when all digital effects are turned off.

When the digital world wakes, the visitor should think:

> **There are creatures living in this room.**

Not:

> Someone projected an ocean video over a bedroom.

The distinction is mandatory.

---

The current room base is visually polished but too close to a styled interior photograph.

REMOVE from the next room artwork:

# 18. Fish Home Behavior

Fish should have specific places they prefer.

Examples:

```text
FISH_HOME_A =
behind Reef Accent

FISH_HOME_B =
bed lower edge

FISH_HOME_C =
wall/floor junction

FISH_HOME_D =
curtain bottom edge
```

This makes them feel resident.

---

# 19. Fish Occlusion

Mandatory:

```text
fish pass behind shelf
fish disappear behind bed
fish partly hide behind Reef Accent
fish can move out of sight
```

A fish that never disappears looks like UI.

---

# 20. Fish–Child Interaction

When child touches near a fish:

First contact:

```text
fish stops
turns slightly
observes
```

Second:

```text
moves 10–30 cm toward touch
```

Then:

```text
returns to independent behavior
```

Do not make fish permanently follow the finger.

The child should feel:

> It noticed me.

Not:

> I control the cursor fish.

---

# 21. Fish Group Reaction

When Milo enters:

```text
fish notice Milo
↓
pause
↓
move gently aside
↓
some hide
```

This creates world hierarchy.

Milo is the room's primary resident.

---

# 22. Milo Species Direction

Digital Milo should be:

```text
juvenile blue-whale-inspired
anatomically believable
rounded gentle proportions
large expressive eye without cartoon exaggeration
soft slate blue-grey
warm ivory underside
subtle natural skin texture
```

Not:

```text
Pixar clone
generic mascot whale
hyper-real adult whale
plastic 3D model
```

---

# 23. Milo Scale

Milo should feel larger than the baby fish but plausible within the magic logic.

Normal apparent length:

```text
80–130 cm
```

Signature event may briefly increase perceived scale.

Do not make Milo permanently enormous.

---

# 24. Milo Lighting

Milo must inherit light from the real room.

When near Shell Light:

```text
warm underside reflection
```

When near floor:

```text
subtle floor glow / shadow
```

When behind bed:

```text
proper occlusion
```

When entering darker area:

```text
reduce exposure
```

This is mandatory for realism.

---

# 25. Contact-Light System

Every creature should create a very weak environmental light response.

For Milo:

```text
soft blue-grey reflected glow
5–12% opacity
large blur
```

For fish:

```text
very small local reflection
```

For Shell:

```text
warm pearl glow
```

Avoid neon bloom.

---

# 26. Shadow / Presence Cue

Do not create hard shadows for swimming creatures.

Instead use:

```text
soft contact-darkening
subtle local caustic displacement
ambient light interaction
```

Goal:

> creature feels inside the room, not pasted over it.

---

# 27. Milo Idle Behavior

Milo must not be permanently visible.

Typical 45-second idle cycle:

```text
0–12 sec:
absent

12–18 sec:
partial appearance near shelf / bed

18–28 sec:
slow swim

28–33 sec:
stop and observe

33–40 sec:
leave

40–45 sec:
room quiet
```

Randomize within controlled bounds.

---

# 87. Frozen Doctrine

> **The room does not become an ocean. The child discovers that something is living in the room.**

> **Empty space is part of the magic.**

> **Three baby fish are more believable than thirty fish.**

> **A creature that stops and watches feels more alive than one that moves forever.**

> **Milo should be discovered, not announced.**

> **Furniture supports the illusion; it must never compete with it.**

> **The only objects allowed to look like MyNest products are Milo, Shell and Reef.**

> **At bedtime, the digital world leaves. Milo stays.**

> **The strongest final frame is not the most magical frame—it is a nearly normal room with a physical Milo sleeping beside the child.**
