# 1. Core Directive

The current prototype explains the concept successfully but does not yet create enough immediate visual attraction.

The next version must prioritize:

> **MAGIC BEFORE EXPLANATION**

A child opening the page should understand almost nothing about the internal system and still want to touch the screen.

A parent should understand within seconds:

> This is a real child's bedroom becoming a responsive world.

Do not lead with:

```text
L0
8 engine states
world engine
projection mapping
pilot protocol
methodology
```

Those remain available deeper in the page.

---

# 2. Primary Experience Gate

The v0.2 experience is successful only if a first-time visitor can understand the basic product without reading explanatory text.

Target sequence:

```text
0–2 sec:
REAL ROOM

2–5 sec:
ROOM BEGINS TO CHANGE

5–8 sec:
MILO ENTERS AND RESPECTS ROOM GEOMETRY

8–12 sec:
CHILD CAN AFFECT THE WORLD

12–18 sec:
ROOM TRANSITIONS TOWARD MOON OCEAN
```

The first 18 seconds are the highest-priority asset in the entire prototype.

# 4. First Screen

The page must open into a believable child's room.

No abstract ocean background.

Required visible room elements:

```text
single child bed
bedside table
small lamp
low bookshelf
window
curtain
plain main wall
visible ceiling
one physical room-friend object
```

Recommended composition:

```text
BED = lower-right or lower-center
WINDOW = left
BOOKSHELF = rear wall
LAMP = bedside
CEILING = visible upper third
```

The room must clearly read as:

> a real bedroom

before any magic starts.

---

# 5. Real-Room Visual Style

Target visual direction:

```text
premium
warm
cinematic
credible
soft
child-friendly
not babyish
not game-like
```

Avoid:

```text
cheap cartoon bedroom
flat illustration
generic stock room
over-saturated blue
gaming UI
neon sci-fi panels
floating cards
```

The room should feel photographically believable even if constructed in 3D or composited.

---

# 6. Ocean Wake Sequence

Trigger:

```text
automatic after first load
OR
single large child-friendly control:
WAKE MY ROOM
```

Preferred first visit:

```text
1–1.5 sec quiet real room
then automatic subtle wake
```

Do not require the child to understand a button before seeing magic.

Sequence:

### Stage A — First Ripple

A faint moving water reflection appears near the wall / bedside area.

Keep it subtle.

---

### Stage B — Water Climbs

The reflected water light slowly travels:

```text
wall
→ curtain edge
→ ceiling
```

Do not flood the entire room instantly.

The transformation should feel discovered.

---

### Stage C — Ceiling Becomes Surface

Create the illusion that the child is below the ocean surface.

Required:

```text
slow caustic light
water-surface motion
depth
soft blue/cyan transition
```

No rapid motion.

---

### Stage D — Small Life Appears

Fish emerge from believable room boundaries:

```text
behind bookshelf
along wall edge
around curtain
```

They should not simply overlay the entire viewport.

---

# 7. Room Geometry Rule

All virtual elements must behave as if physical room geometry exists.

Critical rule:

> **Objects do not ignore the furniture.**

Examples:

```text
fish disappear behind shelf
Milo passes behind bed headboard
light wraps around wall edge
character emerges from behind curtain
shadow changes near furniture
```

This is the main difference between:

```text
animated wallpaper
```

and:

```text
responsive room
```

---

# 8. Milo Entrance

Milo is the first hero IP character.

Do not introduce multiple characters before Milo becomes memorable.

Milo entrance sequence:

```text
small shadow / silhouette first
↓
tail passes near window
↓
Milo enters from left or behind curtain
↓
crosses main wall slowly
↓
passes behind or above furniture
↓
approaches bed area
↓
looks toward user
```

Duration:

```text
4–7 seconds
```

Do not make Milo move quickly.

Milo should feel:

```text
large
gentle
curious
safe
recognizable
```

---

# 9. Milo Visual Requirements

Milo must have:

```text
distinct silhouette
recognizable eye/face
simple body markings
consistent color identity
slow signature motion
```

Avoid:

```text
generic blue whale asset
hyper-real frightening whale
overly childish mascot
complex detailed texture that fails at projection scale
```

The character must remain recognizable in:

```text
website demo
projected wall
social video
future plush
small thumbnail
```


---

# 11. Bed Interaction

Child taps/clicks bed.

Result:

```text
Milo changes path
↓
approaches bed
↓
small bubbles / fish gather
↓
brief soft acknowledgement
```

No modal.

No text required.

---

# 12. Lamp Interaction

Child taps/clicks lamp.

Result:

```text
lamp glow changes
↓
Nini-like ocean glow appears
↓
room shifts slightly warmer / cooler depending state
```

This demonstrates:

> digital world affecting a real room object.

Even if simulated in-browser, the illusion must be clear.

---

# 13. Bookshelf Interaction

Child taps/clicks shelf.

Result:

```text
fish disappear behind shelf
↓
Pip or a small creature appears
↓
short hidden discovery event
```

This gives the child a reason to explore.

---

# 14. Milo Interaction

Child taps Milo.

Milo should:

```text
turn
slow down
approach
make a small circle
release bubbles
```

No speech required initially.

If sound is used:

```text
very short soft whale tone
```

Do not use loud or startling sound.

---

# 15. Touch / Pointer Behavior

Desktop:

```text

```

Mobile:


Do not simulate full body tracking in v0.2.

This is:

```text

REAL ROOM + MAPPED VISUAL + SIMULATED INTERACTION
```

not L1 computer vision.


# 19. Mode 3 — Rest

This is strategically important.

The room must visibly calm itself.

Sequence:

```text

↓
motion slows
↓
water darkens
↓
Milo moves toward distance / bed
↓
Moon Ray appears
↓
ceiling becomes moonlit surface
↓
room reaches near-still loop
```

This visually communicates:

> MyNest is not only entertainment.

# 54. Frozen Product Doctrine

> **The child should see the room before seeing the product.**

> **The magic must begin before the explanation.**

> **Milo must live in the room, not float on top of it.**

> **Interaction must happen through the room, not through a control panel.**

> **The same world that attracts the child must know how to become quiet.**

> **If the first 10 seconds are not compelling, do not add more features. Improve the first 10 seconds.**

> **MyNest v0.2 succeeds when a child sees the room, touches the screen, notices Milo, and wants to see what happens next.**
